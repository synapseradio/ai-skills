# invert-the-problem

Turn a problem inside out — ask how to guarantee the opposite, list every failure path, then reverse each into a protective measure — to reveal hidden structure.

## Worth running when

- Every direct attempt at a goal meets resistance and you can't see why.
- Listing what would make things worse comes more easily than listing what would help.
- You suspect the way the goal is phrased is part of the problem.

## What it hands back

The goal flipped into its opposite, every way to guarantee that opposite, each failure path reversed into a protection, further reversals (of sequence, of who acts on whom, of the target, of the point of view), and the one insight that reorganises the rest.

Excerpts from a run on "How do I get my toddler to eat broccoli?" ([full run](https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills/blob/main/examples/thinkies/invert-the-problem/toddler-broccoli.md)). Its claims about toddlers are the run's own reasoning, with no sources; read them as the method at work, not as parenting advice:

> "Get my toddler to eat" makes the parent the actor and the toddler the object. The flipped goal asks what a toddler does when someone tries to make them do something, and a toddler's most reliable move is refusal. So the real target may not be "broccoli eaten" but "broccoli stops being a contest."
>
> […]
>
> Read together, the protections share a shape: remove the audience, remove the stakes, keep the broccoli present.
>
> […]
>
> The reversal of "get my toddler to eat broccoli" that survives is "get myself to stop needing my toddler to eat broccoli today."
