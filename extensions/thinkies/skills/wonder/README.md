# wonder

Open the possibility space through curiosity. Notices the surprising, asks generative questions, follows interesting threads, and treats confusion as a signal about what deserves attention.

## It helps when

- You're about to solve something and haven't yet asked what's strange about it.
- An everyday thing suddenly looks odd, and you want to follow the oddness rather than explain it away.
- You'd rather leave with better questions than with a quick answer.

## What you'll get

What's surprising about the subject, a spread of "what if" and "how might" questions, threads followed from one question to the next, the points of confusion treated as clues, and a closing sort of what seems likely, what's plausible but untested, what's open, and what's unknown, with questions worth carrying forward.

Trimmed from a run on "Why do people talk to their cars?" ([full run](https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills/blob/main/examples/thinkies/wonder/talking-to-cars.md)):

> The surprise underneath all three: an object with no ears receives the same social behavior we give a person, at the very moments when the behavior can do the least.
>
> […]
>
> Testing the question: people also talk to boats, printers, and stubborn jars of pickles. The jar has no face and makes no sound, so the face and the noise cannot be the whole story.
>
> […]
>
> Confusion 1: the talk persists after the speaker knows it is pointless. If it were only a mistake, it would stop once noticed. It does not stop. So the speech must be paying for itself in some way other than being heard.

## Install

```bash
npx skills add https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills
```

Or copy `skills/thinkies/wonder/` into `~/.claude/skills/wonder/`.

## Usage

```
/wonder <situation or problem>
```
