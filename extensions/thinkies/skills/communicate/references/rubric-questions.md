# Rubric questions

Questions beyond the baseline, one section per ring of the wheel in [align](./align.md). In step 3, copy the section for each ring where Diagnose found risks, keeping the questions that bear on those risks. Answer each in Score in your own words, pointing to the draft. "Nothing here" is an ordinary answer.

## 1. Incitement

- What, if anything, would go missing for the audience, as the draft stands, if the piece were never written?
- What, if anything, does the draft insist on?
- What, if anything, in the draft ties the occasion to the audience's own concerns?

## 2. Setting

- What relation between the writer and the audience does the draft's tone take, such as warmth or distance, formality or ease?
- How, if at all, does the place where the piece will be met, such as a wall, a courtroom, a feed, or a log, show in the draft?
- Where the piece speaks to several people at once, how does each passage stand for each of them?

## 3. Audience and purpose

- What foundations, if any, such as a term, a concept, or an earlier event, does the draft take the audience to bring?
- How does the draft's vocabulary stand beside the audience's own?
- How does the depth of each explanation sit against what the audience already holds?
- What does the draft take the audience to be after, such as quick information, foundations, words to think with, or comfort?
- How does each idea new to this audience reach them, such as by a definition, an example, an analogy, or its place in the order?
- How does the draft show who acts, at each action whose actor this audience needs to know?

## 4. Point

- In what state does the draft leave its audience where it stops, such as informed, moved, ready to act, drawn into a question, witnessed, or bound?
- Where, if anywhere, in the draft does the audience reach the point?
- If the audience accepts what the draft brings them to, what else, if anything, do they take on with it?
- How does the ending stand toward whatever question or need the opening raised, if it raised one?
- Where the draft leaves something unsaid for the audience to complete, what direction does it give them?

## 5. Material

- What does each example in the draft put in the foreground, such as the relations that define the idea, or features like style, era, or tone?
- What instance, if any, stands beside each category word the draft uses, such as leadership, community, or resilience?
- Where an analogy stands, which parts map onto which, and where does the mapping stop?
- What stands behind each quantity or time word the audience will act on, such as many or soon: a number, a date, a comparison, or a standard the form leaves open?
- By what process, as the draft tells it, does each effect it asserts come about?

## 6. Form

- How does the shape the draft takes on the page, such as argument, exposition, exploration, instruction, evocation, narration, or binding, sit with the purpose recorded?
- What ordering principle does the draft follow, such as dependency, chronology, priority, dialectic, parallel restatement, or a delayed turn?
- What does the draft promise its audience it is, such as an account, a story, a performance, or an instrument, and how does each passage stand toward that promise?
- What does each question in the draft do for its audience?
- Where the draft follows a tradition its audience may not expect, what, if anything, bridges the two?

## 7. Voice

- Whom does the draft sound like, passage by passage?
- From what relation to the subject does the voice speak: inside it, beside it, or outside it?
- On what does the voice rest its authority, as the draft shows it?
- What connotation does each significant word carry beside its sense?
- What brings each recurring word back into the draft, such as the subject, the form, or the voice?
- Whom, if anyone, does each qualifying phrase in the draft serve?
- How, if at all, does the emotional pitch move across the draft?
- How, if at all, does sentence length change from one sentence to the next?
- On what clause does each long sentence end?
- Read aloud at speaking pace, where, if anywhere, do breath and punctuation part?

## 8. Extent

- What, if anything, does each paragraph do that the one before it does not?
- What, if anything, does each section rest on that arrives earlier or later in the draft?
- Which phrases, if any, could be cut with nothing lost?
- How long does the draft hold each question it opens before it answers, if it answers?
- What question and answer, if any, does each section carry of its own?
