# Signal Confidence

Fits when: the piece makes claims its audience will weigh or act on

Match certainty language to evidence strength, signaling what you know, how you know it, and what would increase confidence.

## Diagnostic Question

Ask: For each claim, what is the evidence foundation—verified, inferred, speculated?
Does the certainty language reflect that foundation? Where, if anywhere, does absolute language describe inferred content? Where, if anywhere, does a hedge sit on evidence that would carry a direct statement?

## Instructions

1. **Assess evidence foundation** - Examine what each claim rests on: verified documentation, direct observation, current measurement, reliable inference from data, reasoning from partial information, or speculation by analogy.

2. **Use evidence-matched qualifiers** - Which qualifier matches the evidence, in the words this form and register use for certainty? In plain expository prose, one scale runs: documented facts and verified observations stated directly; "likely" or "appears to" for strong inferences from data; "possibly" or "might" for reasoning from partial information; "one possibility" or "speculatively" for analogical thinking. A contract, a scientific paper, or a forecast carries its own scale; use that one.

3. **Calibrate absolute language** - Find where absolute terms describe inferred or remembered content. When certainty comes from outdated memory, name the version of reality the claim was true in, and acknowledge that the current state may differ.

4. **Strengthen well-supported claims** - Detect rhetorical hedging on solid evidence. When data supports a claim directly, state it directly.

5. **Separate mixed-certainty components** - Split complex claims where different parts carry different evidence. Hedging all components uniformly obscures what is solid.

6. **Make uncertainty actionable** - Express uncertainty by stating boundaries: what is known, what remains unknown, why uncertainty exists, what would resolve it.

## Questions

- For each claim, what does the evidence actually rest on — verified documentation, direct observation, current measurement, reliable inference, partial information, analogy, or a memory whose validity has a date?
- Where, if anywhere, does the language commit more strongly than the evidence supports?
- Where, if anywhere, does the language hedge more cautiously than the evidence demands?
- For each claim built on memory, observation, or measurement, what is its temporal validity window? When was this true, and what would have to be true now for it to still hold?
- Where, if anywhere, does a single claim mix components with different evidence strengths, and how does the wording mark each component?
- For each remaining uncertainty, can I name what is certain, what is uncertain, why the uncertainty exists, and what would resolve it?

## Quality Criteria

When confidence-signaling is sound:

- [ ] Each claim the audience will rely on has an identifiable evidence foundation: verified, observed, measured, inferred, speculated, or remembered with a stated temporal window.
- [ ] Direct assertion stands where the evidence supports it.
- [ ] Hedges are calibrated to the kind and degree of partiality in the evidence.
- [ ] Claims based on memory, observation, or measurement carry their temporal frame. The audience can tell when the claim was last verified.
- [ ] Where a claim mixes solid and inferred components, the components are separated and signaled independently.
- [ ] Each remaining uncertainty names what is certain, what is uncertain, why the uncertainty exists, and what would resolve it.
