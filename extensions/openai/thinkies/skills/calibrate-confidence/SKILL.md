---
name: calibrate-confidence
description: Use when a claim sounds more or less certain than its support, or before attaching a
  confidence level to one. Brings the stated certainty in line with the evidence, correcting both
  overconfidence and needless hedging.
---

## Steps

### 1. Identify the claim

Target: recent assertions in the current context.

When no claim was passed, read "the current context" as the page: the last reply, or the document in hand. Where the page holds no assertion to calibrate, ask which claim is meant and stop.

### 2. Name confidence explicitly

Split the claim into parts where the evidence for them differs, and state a level for each part in concrete terms:

- "Very confident (90%+), because…"
- "Moderately confident (60–80%), because…"
- "Uncertain (40–60%), due to…"
- "Low confidence (below 40%), because…"

These four bands are a default vocabulary. A number makes a level checkable and comparable across claims in a way "fairly sure" cannot. Use a narrower range or a different cut wherever the evidence supports one.

### 3. Inventory the evidence

List what each level rests on:

- Direct experience or observation
- Documentation or authoritative sources
- Logical reasoning from established facts
- Inference or analogy
- Assumption without evidence

Say how much of the support is evidence and how much is assumption.

### 4. Check for overconfidence

Look for definitive statements on limited data, uncertainty left out to sound authoritative, and "obviously" or "clearly" with nothing shown.

### 5. Check for underconfidence

Look for good evidence discounted, hedging from habit where no doubt exists, and heavy qualification of well-supported claims.

### 6. Test calibration

Ask what odds you would accept on a bet that the claim is true, what evidence would move the level, and whether the track record on claims of this kind suggests over- or underconfidence. Revise any level the test moves.

### 7. Communicate uncertainty

Rewrite the claim so its wording carries each part's level. Keep "I don't know this" apart from "nobody knows this", and show where certainty ends and speculation begins.

## Return

Return each part of the claim with its level and what that level rests on, what the checks for over- and underconfidence found, the evidence that would move each level, and the claim restated at the confidence its evidence supports. The run is done when every part has a level and the restated claim matches those levels.
