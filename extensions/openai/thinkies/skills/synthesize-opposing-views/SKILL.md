---
name: synthesize-opposing-views
description: Use when two positions directly contradict and picking a side would lose something. Takes
  each at full strength and builds a third position that keeps what both got right and explains why
  they conflicted.
---

Resolve two positions that contradict each other into a third that explains what each got right. The synthesis is worth only as much as the weaker side's statement, so each position is built to full strength before the conflict is examined.

### 1. Identify the opposing positions

Target: the thesis and antithesis in the current context. Where the request itself states the positions, take them from there. Where only one side is given, build the strongest opposing position yourself and say that it is yours.

### 2. State the thesis clearly

Its core arguments, what it explains well, its strengths and appeal.

### 3. Develop the antithesis

Present it as a genuine alternative, not mere negation: its own arguments, what it explains well, what it reveals that the thesis missed.

### 4. Examine the conflict

Where exactly do they contradict? What does each get right or miss? Are they answering different questions or operating on different assumptions?

### 5. Identify the deeper truth

What higher-level understanding resolves the contradiction? Is it a false dichotomy? Are both partially right in different contexts? What does the conflict itself reveal?

### 6. Construct the synthesis

Build a new position that incorporates insights from both, transcends the either/or framing, and explains what neither alone could.

### 7. Test the synthesis

Does it account for what each side got right and resolve the original conflict? What new tensions or questions does it create? Where it fails the test, return to step 5.

## What to return

Lead with the synthesis as a position someone could hold and argue for, in a sentence or two. Follow it with what it keeps from each side, the point where the two contradicted, and the new tensions step 7 found. Where the two positions turn out to answer different questions, or no position resolves them, say that in place of a synthesis; a split-the-difference answer would hide the conflict from the reader.
