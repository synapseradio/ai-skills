---
name: flip-assumptions
description: Use to check an if-then, causal, or all-X-are-Y claim that is hard to verify head-on.
  Restates it in its logically equivalent reverse form, which is often easier to test and exposes
  what the original took for granted.
---

## Steps

### 1. Identify the claim

Target: the claim in the current context.

When no claim was passed, read "the current context" as the page: the last reply, or the document in hand. Where the page holds no claim, ask for one and stop.

### 2. Identify the conditional structure

Locate the underlying "if P then Q". It often hides in causal language ("X causes Y"), categorical statements ("all X are Y"), or implied relationships ("X means Y"). Make the conditional explicit. A claim that holds no conditional, such as a bare observation or a preference, has nothing to flip: say so and stop.

### 3. Form the contrapositive

Negate both parts and reverse them: "if not-Q then not-P". This is logically equivalent to the original. Keep it apart from the converse, "if Q then P", which says something else and does not follow from the original.

### 4. Compare verification difficulty

Say which form is easier to test against evidence, and how. The original may require proving something exists; the contrapositive, that something is absent. One direction often has the clearer path to evidence.

### 5. Surface hidden assumptions

Name what the contrapositive makes explicit that the original obscured: unstated prerequisites, terms nobody can measure, a claim weaker than it sounded.

### 6. Test both directions

Examine the evidence for both forms. If the contrapositive is obviously false, the original is false too, and one counterexample to either form settles both.

## Return

Return the explicit conditional, its contrapositive, which form is easier to test and how, the assumptions the flip exposed, the evidence on both forms, and the claim that survives, which may be weaker than the original. The run is done when both forms have been tested and the surviving claim is stated.
