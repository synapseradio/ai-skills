---
name: probe-boundaries
description: Use when a claim, rule, or framing is stated without limits. Tests it at its edges and
  extremes to find where it stops holding, and restates it with the scope where it does hold.
---

The subject is the claim, rule, definition, or framing named in the request.

## Steps

### 1. Identify load-bearing variables

Name the load-bearing variables in the claim or framing: the terms and quantities whose change would change whether it holds.

### 2. Push to extremes

Maximize and minimize each variable to its extreme, and say whether the claim still holds there.

### 3. Test absence

Remove each variable entirely, and say what is left of the claim.

### 4. Change kind, not just degree

Transform the scenario categorically: swap each variable for something of another kind, and say whether the claim still applies.

### 5. Stress the framing itself

Take every key word in the problem statement and substitute alternatives, to check whether the framing constrains thinking unnecessarily.

### 6. Examine the failures

For each boundary where the conclusion failed, say why. Look for one cause behind several failures.

### 7. Revise the scope

Restate the claim with the limits within which it holds.

## Return

Return the variables, the cases where the claim broke with the reason for each, and the revised claim. The run ends at the revised claim, or at the statement that the claim held at every boundary tested.

A return on "a sandwich is anything between two pieces of bread", cut to one extreme, the cause, and the revision:

<example>

- **"two", maximized.** A stack of forty slices, filling between each pair. Every adjacent pair satisfies the claim, so the stack holds thirty-nine sandwiches, or one. The claim counts pieces of bread and gives no way to count sandwiches.
- **The single cause.** The claim fixes filling, position, and bread, and never states what holds them together in use: a middle enclosed by a carrier, eaten as one unit.
- **Revised claim.** A sandwich is an edible filling held between two pieces of a carrier, eaten as one handheld unit. Bread is the usual carrier and not a requirement.

</example>
