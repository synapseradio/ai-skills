---
name: integrate-other-perspectives
description: Use when several viewpoints, findings, or sources must become one picture. Combines them
  into a coherent whole that keeps what matters from each and settles which of their conflicts are
  real.
---

Build one picture out of several views, so that each view keeps what it contributes and the whole says something none of them says alone. Work on the views as their holders stated them, because a view rewritten to fit loses the part that would have tested the whole.

### 1. Identify the perspectives

Target: the perspectives in the current context. Where the request itself names the views, take them from there. Where only one view is present, say so and ask for the others, or name which views you supplied yourself.

### 2. Lay out what is being combined

List each piece in its own terms — note its origin and individual value. Keep each at full strength at this step.

### 3. Find points of contact

Where do ideas naturally touch, support each other, or fill each other's gaps? What does one explain about another?

### 4. Address tensions

Where do they conflict? Are the conflicts real or apparent? Can both hold in different contexts? What does the tension reveal?

### 5. Build the synthesis

Describe the larger picture they create together: how each piece fits, what emerges that wasn't in any single piece, and the organizing principle that unifies them.

### 6. Test coherence

Does the integration hold together logically, preserve what matters from each piece, create new understanding, and explain simply? Where a test fails, return to step 5.

### 7. Check what's missing

Remaining gaps, what doesn't fit, what new questions arise.

## What to return

Lead with the synthesis from step 5: the organizing principle in a sentence, then where each view sits in the whole. Follow it with the tensions that stayed real after step 4, and what step 7 found missing. A view that fits nowhere is reported as such, since forcing it in would hide a gap from the reader.
