# integrity: a garden-centre moon-planting leaflet

- skill: integrity, from commit 0c9258f
- subject: A garden-centre leaflet: "Planting by the moon calendar doubles your tomato yield. Gardeners have known this for centuries, and our customers report bumper harvests. Buy the 2027 Moon Planner inside."
- kind: single run

## Prompt

/integrity A garden-centre leaflet: "Planting by the moon calendar doubles your tomato yield. Gardeners have known this for centuries, and our customers report bumper harvests. Buy the 2027 Moon Planner inside."

## Run

Target: the leaflet text above. Five claims sit in it, plus one hidden premise. I ran the five lenses over each, and searched the web for the one claim that cites a checkable source.

**Inventory.** Separating what the leaflet asserts:

1. Planting by the moon calendar doubles tomato yield (causal, quantitative).
2. Gardeners have known this for centuries (appeal to tradition, historical).
3. Customers report bumper harvests (testimonial, aggregate).
4. The 2027 Moon Planner is worth buying (implied, commercial).
5. Hidden premise: the customers who report bumper harvests planted by the moon, and would not have had them otherwise.

**Dissection.** Tracing each support chain:

- Claim 1 has no chain. No trial, no comparison group, no source. "Doubles" is a precise number attached to nothing.
- Claim 2 rests on long use, and long use shows a practice persisted, not that it works. Tomatoes have also been grown alongside charms and planting-day superstitions for centuries. The chain ends in the tradition itself.
- Claim 3 rests on self-selected reports. Customers who bought a planner and had a good year are the ones who speak up; customers with a poor year, or with an ordinary year they never compared, do not. A good harvest also has ordinary causes: weather, variety, feeding, watering, pest pressure, and the gardener's attention, which a buyer of a planner plausibly gives more of.
- Claim 4 follows only if 1 holds, and its beneficiary is the seller.
- The hidden premise in 5 is that the moon caused the harvest. Nothing in the leaflet separates that from every other difference between one season and the next.

**Pattern.** Weaknesses present:

- Appeal to tradition (claim 2).
- Post hoc reasoning and survivorship in testimonials (claim 3): a good harvest followed planting by the moon, so the moon is credited.
- Unsourced precision: "doubles" reads as measured and is not.
- Conflict of interest: the claim and the sale are the same sentence, so the evidence is doing commercial work.
- Vague quantifiers: "gardeners", "customers", "bumper".

**Calibration.** The external evidence I could reach, ranked:

- Highest rung reached: a published literature review, Mayoral et al., "What Has Been Thought and Taught on the Lunar Influence on Plants in Agriculture? Perspective from Physics and Biology", Agronomy, 2020, <https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy10070955>. Its abstract, seen through search results, states: "We found that there is no reliable, science-based evidence for any relationship between lunar phases and plant physiology in any plant-science related textbooks or peer-reviewed journal articles justifying agricultural practices conditioned by the lunar cycle." I read the abstract text as returned by search, not the full paper. That is enough to support "no reliable evidence found in the literature reviewed", and not enough to support "proven false for tomatoes".
- Lower rung, not relied on: a garden-advice page (<https://gardenuk.co.uk>, "Lunar Gardening UK: Planting by the Moon") cites a Florida lima-bean trial and a UK onion trial. I did not open either trial, and the page is a secondary account, so I use it as a lead and cite nothing from it.
- The other pages that came back (biodynamic associations, gardening blogs) describe the calendar's tradition and rules. They show what the calendar is, and none supplies a controlled yield comparison I could open.

What the evidence warrants, per claim:

- Claim 1: unsupported by anything in the leaflet, and contradicted in direction by the review's finding. A doubling is an extreme figure, since a doubling would be among the largest effects any single planting practice could have on a tomato crop, and an effect that large would leave a trace in the trials the review searched. That last step is my inference, not a finding of the review.
- Claim 2: true as a description of tradition, irrelevant to whether the practice works.
- Claim 3: plausibly true as a report, uninformative as evidence.
- Claim 4: no support; it depends on claim 1.

**Resolution.** Aligning wording with support:

- "Planting by the moon calendar doubles your tomato yield" becomes "Some gardeners plant by the moon calendar." That is all the leaflet supports.
- "Gardeners have known this for centuries" becomes "Gardeners have planted by the moon for centuries."
- "Our customers report bumper harvests" becomes "Some of our customers report good harvests," and would need a count and a comparison to say more.
- "Buy the 2027 Moon Planner" stands as a sale, with no claim attached.

## Verification Summary

### Claims Verified

- Gardeners have planted by the moon for centuries: the practice is documented in the biodynamic sowing and planting calendar tradition, described on the biodynamic association pages the search returned. The historical persistence is supported. That this shows the practice works is not.
- The leaflet is selling a product: the text itself says "Buy the 2027 Moon Planner inside".

### Claims Requiring Attention

- "Planting by the moon calendar doubles your tomato yield": no source in the leaflet, and a 2020 literature review (<https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy10070955>) found no reliable science-based evidence linking lunar phases to plant physiology. The figure "doubles" is unsourced precision.
- "Gardeners have known this for centuries": appeal to tradition. Age shows persistence, not efficacy.
- "Customers report bumper harvests": self-selected testimonials with no comparison group. Weather, variety, feeding, watering, and gardener attention all offer competing explanations. The leaflet gives no count.
- Hidden premise, that the moon caused the harvests: nothing in the text separates it from every other difference between seasons.
- Commercial interest: the claim and the sale share a sentence.

### Acknowledged Uncertainty

- I read the review's abstract as returned by search, not the full paper, so what it did and did not test on tomatoes specifically is unverified by me.
- I did not open the two trials cited on the garden-advice page, so I give no weight to their figures.
- Whether some customers really did get larger harvests is unknown. I have no data on them, and the leaflet gives none.
- Whether a moon planner has some indirect benefit, such as prompting a gardener to plan sowing dates and attend to the plot, is untested here. That effect, if it exists, would come from the planning and not from the moon.
- This report surfaces issues for human assessment; it does not guarantee that the review's conclusion is the last word.

Significant issues were found. I can revise the leaflet with the corrections above applied: the supported wording, with the yield figure and the testimonial claim removed or replaced by something the garden centre can source.
