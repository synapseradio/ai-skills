**Subject:** The instruction "Fix the office kitchen.", given as a task with no stated fault, in a shared workplace kitchen.

1. What act must "Fix the office kitchen." support (decide, explain, build, or fix), and at what level is it stated (why, what, how, or which one)?

   The act is fix: the verb is in the sentence, and someone is meant to end with a kitchen that works. The level is what. It names a thing to be repaired and leaves out why it matters, how to repair it, and which part is broken. "Fix" hides a further step, because a fix needs a fault, and the sentence does not name one.

2. How does "Fix the office kitchen." read a few rungs above and a few rungs below its current level, one line per rung?

   Above:

   - Kind-of rung: Repair one of the shared facilities the office runs on.
   - Why rung: People who eat, drink, and gather at work need a place where that is easy, so that lunch and coffee do not cost them time or goodwill.
   - Why-of-why rung: The office keeps people able to work together through the day without friction they have to manage.

   Below:

   - How rung: Find what stops the kitchen from doing its job, then repair or replace that part.
   - Which-fault rung: The dishwasher no longer drains, the fridge is warm, the sink drips, or the bin overflows because nobody owns emptying it.
   - Which-instance rung: The dishwasher on the left drains slowly because its filter is clogged, and a screwdriver and ten minutes clear it.

3. What does each of those rungs let you do, and what detail does each bury or lack?

   - Office keeps people working together (top): lets you decide whether a kitchen is worth attention at all, against other office concerns. It buries every actual fault and gives nothing to repair.
   - People need an easy place to eat and gather: lets you judge the outcome, since a fix works if lunch and coffee stop costing time or goodwill. It buries the mechanism, and it hides that a broken habit fixes differently from a broken machine.
   - Repair a shared facility: lets you set a repair routine, with a place to report faults and someone to hear them. It buries this particular kitchen and what its faults are.
   - Find what stops the kitchen and repair it (the given level, one rung down): lets you start an inspection. It lacks the fault, so it cannot yet name a tool, a cost, or a person.
   - The fault is one of the dishwasher, fridge, sink, or bin: lets you triage, checking each in turn and ranking by how many people it blocks. It buries the cause inside each, and it assumes the fault is a thing and not a habit.
   - This dishwasher, this clogged filter: lets you act now with a screwdriver. It lacks everything else: if the dishwasher is the wrong fault, the detail is wasted, and it says nothing about the next breakage.

4. Which rung do you act at to fix the office kitchen, given the least detail that still supports the act?

   Act at the find-what-stops-the-kitchen rung, then descend one rung to the fault list once the fault is seen. That is the least detail that still supports a fix: "repair a shared facility" cannot tell anyone what to pick up, and "this filter" cannot be chosen until someone has looked. To explain a fault, the skill says to work up from the lower rungs. The fault is not known yet, so the first move is to look, at the lower rungs, for what fails, and to let that decide the rung you settle on. Once a fault turns up, act at its instance rung if the fault is a machine. If the fault is a habit, such as a bin nobody empties, act at the rung above the machine ("who owns this task"), because a repair at the instance rung, one emptied bin, undoes itself by Friday.

   Open: which part of the kitchen is failing, and whether the failure is a machine or a habit. The answer to question 4 changes the moment that is known, and this set does not give it.
