**Subject:** Six half-ideas for a family board-game night, taken together as one design for a night in which nobody quits in tears: teams instead of every-player-for-themselves, a snack break every 30 minutes, the youngest player picking the first game, a "no gloating" jar, one cooperative game per night, and a prize for the funniest loss.

1. What does each of the six ideas do on its own (teams, the 30-minute snack break, the youngest picking the first game, the "no gloating" jar, one cooperative game per night, the prize for the funniest loss), and which of them cannot yet run on its own?

   - Teams: a loss belongs to two or more people instead of one, and a win is shared. Runs on its own once there is a way to split the players.
   - Snack break every 30 minutes: stops play on a clock, whatever the score, and gives everyone something else to do for a few minutes. Runs on its own; it needs only a timer.
   - The youngest picks the first game: gives one player, the one least likely to be chosen for anything, the opening decision. Runs on its own.
   - "No gloating" jar: makes a winner's crowing cost something. It cannot yet run alone. It needs a definition of gloating, someone to rule on it, and a price to put in the jar.
   - One cooperative game per night: for the length of one game, everyone wins or loses together against the game. Runs on its own once a cooperative game is chosen.
   - Prize for the funniest loss: makes losing well a way of winning something. It cannot yet run alone. It needs someone to judge funniest, a prize, and a moment to award it.

2. For each pair of the six ideas that touches, what passes between them or holds them together in a board-game night, and which pairs have nothing between them?

   Pairs that touch:

   - Teams and the jar: teams change who the winner is, so the jar has to catch a team's gloating, not one person's. Teams feed the jar its target.
   - Teams and the funniest-loss prize: the prize needs a unit to award. If it goes to a person, the losing team's members compete for it; if it goes to a team, the team shares the laugh.
   - Teams and the cooperative game: the cooperative game is teams taken to the limit, one team of everyone. It sits at the end of the same spectrum, and the two share the same thread of belonging to the group.
   - The jar and the funniest-loss prize: they hold the two ends of an outcome. The jar restrains the winner; the prize lifts the loser. Together they cover both sides of every result.
   - The snack break and every game idea: the break constrains all play. It comes after a fixed stretch, so it falls after some loss and before the next round, and gives a stung player a reason to stand up and leave the table without quitting.
   - The youngest's pick and the cooperative game: the pick comes first in the night and the cooperative game is one later slot, so the first choice fixes what the rest of the night has to fit around.

   Pairs with nothing between them, left unconnected:

   - The snack break and the youngest's pick.
   - The jar and the cooperative game (a game with no opponent leaves nothing to gloat over).
   - The cooperative game and the funniest-loss prize (in a cooperative game the loss is shared, so nobody's loss stands out to judge).

3. What does the arrangement of the six ideas do for a board-game night that no single idea does?

   It gives every outcome a soft landing. A loser is cushioned by a team, a snack break, or a chance at the prize; a winner is restrained by the jar; and one game in the night has no loser at all. No single idea covers both sides of a result and the time between results. Teams cushion the loser but leave the winner free to crow. The jar restrains the winner but leaves the loser stinging. The break covers the time between games but does nothing about what happened in them. The property is a night in which a loss never lands on one person, in front of everyone, with nothing to do next.

4. Which of the six ideas does that property need, and which one does the night work without when removed?

   - Teams: remove them and each loss lands on one person again. The property weakens at its base. Needed.
   - Snack break: remove it and a stung player has no natural exit before the mood sets. Needed.
   - "No gloating" jar: remove it and the winner's side of every result goes unrestrained. Needed.
   - One cooperative game: remove it and the night no longer contains a stretch where nobody loses to anybody. The property survives in the competitive games but the night loses its guaranteed no-loser stretch. Needed, at the least strength of the four.
   - Prize for the funniest loss: remove it and losing has no upside. The loser is cushioned by teams and the break but not given anything to gain. Needed.
   - The youngest picks the first game: remove it and the property does not change. A loss lands the same way whoever chose the game. This idea does not belong to this whole. It belongs to a different one, about who gets a say, and it can stay in the night as a courtesy that does none of this night's work.

5. What does that property need in a board-game night that none of the six ideas supplies?

   Four missing parts:

   - A way to form teams, and to change them from game to game. Without rotation the same people lose together every game and the cushion turns into a fixed side.
   - A ruling on gloating: a definition of it, a person or rule that decides, and a price. Without them the jar is a jar with nothing in it, and an argument over what counted takes its place.
   - A way to leave without quitting: a step-out any player may take at any time, with no explanation owed and no penalty. The snack break covers one moment on a clock; a tearful player needs an exit at any moment.
   - A judge and a prize for the funniest loss, and a moment to award it. The prize idea names the reward but supplies neither.

6. What is the whole: the family board-game night made of these parts, its main arrangement, and what it does?

   It is a board-game night in which teams that rotate between games, a snack break on a 30-minute clock, a jar that fines the winner's crowing, and a prize for the funniest loss surround each competitive game, with one cooperative game among them, so that every result has a soft landing for the loser, a limit on the winner, and a break before the next round; and the youngest's first pick stays as a courtesy outside that work.
