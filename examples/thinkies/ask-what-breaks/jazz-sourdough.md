# ask-what-breaks: jazz and a thriving sourdough starter

- skill: ask-what-breaks, from commit 0c9258f
- subject: Our sourdough starter is thriving because we play it jazz.
- kind: single run

## Prompt

/ask-what-breaks Our sourdough starter is thriving because we play it jazz.

## Run

### 1. The claim under test

As stated, the claim is vague in three places: "thriving", "because", and "jazz". Each needs a boundary before anything can defeat it.

**Falsifiable form:** Over the period in which the household has played jazz near the starter, the starter's activity (rise height, time to peak, bubble structure, sourness, recovery after feeding) has been better than it would have been over the same period without the jazz, and the sound is what made the difference.

The claim has two parts, and they fail differently:

- **Observation:** the starter is thriving. It is a state, and can be checked against a baseline.
- **Causal claim:** the jazz produced the thriving. It is a comparison with a world that did not happen, and can only be checked by building that world.

The rest of this run treats them separately.

### 2. Defeater types in play

- **Undercutting** defeaters attack how we got from "we play jazz and the starter is thriving" to "the jazz caused it". They leave the thriving intact and remove the jazz's credit.
- **Rebutting** defeaters attack the conclusion directly. They are observations that would show the starter is not thriving, or that jazz does the opposite of helping.

The chain under attack:

1. The starter is thriving. (observation)
2. The jazz has been playing during that time. (co-occurrence)
3. Nothing else that matters differs from the times before or without jazz. (no confounders)
4. Sound can reach the culture and affect it. (mechanism exists)
5. Therefore the jazz is why it is thriving. (inference)

### 3. Undercutting defeaters, link by link

**Link 1: the starter is thriving.**

- "Thriving" may have no baseline. If there was no record of rise and peak time before the jazz, "thriving" means "it looks good to us", and looking good is not measured.
- The judgment may be relative to a bad stretch. A starter recovering from neglect looks like thriving next to its own slump, with jazz playing throughout the recovery.
- One vessel, one starter, one household. A single culture has no spread, so an ordinary good week and a jazz effect look the same.

**Link 2: the jazz was playing during it.**

- "We play it jazz" may be loose. If the music plays in the kitchen most days, the starter is exposed almost continuously, and there is no jazz-free stretch to compare against. Without variation in the supposed cause, there is nothing to correlate.
- If the jazz began at the same moment as the good results, two things changed together (see link 3), and if it began well before, the timing does not line up with the improvement.

**Link 3: nothing else differs. This is the weakest link, and the most likely place for the claim to break.**

Whoever plays a starter jazz is probably also the kind of person who looks after it. Candidate hidden dependencies, each of which would produce a thriving starter with the jazz doing nothing:

- **Attention.** Someone who talks and plays music to a starter is around it, feeds it on schedule, and notices problems early. Schedule regularity is a well-understood driver of starter health.
- **Feeding ratio and flour.** A switch to whole grain, rye, or a higher feeding ratio at any point in the period would change activity a great deal.
- **Temperature.** A kitchen that is warm because people are in it, cooking, with the music on, is warmer than an empty one. Fermentation rate follows temperature steeply. Music playing means people present means heat.
- **Water.** A change in tap water, filtered against unfiltered, or chlorine level, changes how a culture behaves.
- **Season.** Spring and summer kitchens are warmer than winter ones. If the jazz began in a season of warming, the season is a sufficient explanation.
- **Vessel and lid.** A new jar, a looser lid, or more frequent stirring changes oxygen exposure.
- **Regression to the mean.** People start a ritual such as jazz when a starter is doing poorly and keep it up after it recovers on its own. The ritual then collects credit for a recovery that was coming anyway.
- **Selection of memory.** Good days with music are remembered as confirmation. Good days without music, and bad days with it, are not counted.

**Link 4: sound can affect the culture.**

- The starter is a community of yeasts and lactic acid bacteria in flour and water. It has no ears. Any effect of sound would have to be physical: vibration transmitted through the counter, the jar, or the air, changing something about the culture's environment.
- I cannot cite a controlled result here. I did not run a search for this run, so the claim that microbial responses to audible music are weak or unestablished is my own assessment, unverified. Finding a published controlled study would move this link in either direction, and is a step for whoever wants to act on the answer.
- Even if vibration mattered, "jazz" would not be the operative variable. Volume, bass frequency, and the speaker's contact with the surface would be. Genre would be an incidental label for those. A claim that jazz specifically helps needs a reason why jazz differs from other sound at equal volume and frequency, and nothing in the stated claim supplies one.

**Link 5: the inference.**

- This is post hoc reasoning: the jazz preceded the thriving, so the jazz caused it. Precedence is what every confounder above would also produce.
- The claim quietly assumes the outcome is a property of the starter under the treatment. Starters vary from day to day without any treatment, so a run of good days does not need a cause.
- The claim is unfalsified as stated. If a bad day arrives, "it missed the jazz" or "it did not like the piece" would rescue it. A claim that survives every outcome tells us nothing about the world.

### 4. Rebutting defeaters

Each is stated as "seeing X would defeat this", with the experiment that produces X.

- **Seeing an unmusical starter match it.** Split the starter into two matched jars: same flour, same ratio, same feeding time, same temperature, same lid, the same shelf if possible. One jar gets jazz, one gets silence, over at least several feeding cycles. If the silent jar's rise height and time to peak match the jazz jar's within the day-to-day spread you see in either jar alone, the claim is defeated.
- **Seeing the effect reverse under swapped conditions.** Swap which jar hears the music halfway through. If the thriving follows the jar rather than the music, something about the jar, its history or its culture, explains it. If it follows the music, the claim gains real support.
- **Seeing a different sound do as well.** Play the jar something else at equal volume: a podcast, static, a bass tone. If any sound produces the same result, the effect (if there is one) belongs to vibration or to human presence, not to jazz.
- **Seeing no effect at low temperature.** Hold the jazz jar in a cool room, with music, next to a warm silent jar. If the warm silent jar is more active, temperature dominates, and the size of the gap gives a rough upper bound on what jazz could be adding.
- **Seeing a dose-response failure.** If louder or longer jazz does nothing, or more jazz makes it worse, the causal story loses its simplest form. A cause with no dose is harder to believe.
- **Seeing thriving continue after the jazz stops.** If the household stops playing music for two weeks and the starter's rise and peak times do not shift, the jazz was not maintaining it.
- **Seeing it fail while the jazz continues.** A single reliably poor batch, such as a starter that sours flat or goes sluggish after a feeding-ratio change while the jazz keeps playing, shows that something other than the jazz can override whatever the jazz does.

None of these is available from the story as told. All are cheap, and none needs equipment beyond two jars, a marker for the jar's level, and a note of the time.

Because these rebutting defeaters are concrete and cheap, the next step follows from them and needs no further deliberation: **do not conclude the jazz is the cause; run the two-jar comparison first, and treat the claim as a hypothesis until it survives it.**

### 5. Defeater strength

| Defeater | What it needs | Type | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No baseline for "thriving" | Nothing recorded before the jazz | Weakener | Revise the observation: measure rise height and time to peak for two weeks before drawing anything from it. The claim survives once measured. |
| No jazz-free stretch | The music plays all the time | Weakener | Correlation cannot be computed. The claim is not defeated, only untestable until the exposure varies. |
| Attention and schedule | A caretaker who talks to the starter is a diligent one | **Destroyer** of the causal claim if the schedule is what changed; weakener otherwise | If regular feeding began with the jazz, the jazz loses its credit entirely and the claim has to be rebuilt from the observation up. |
| Temperature and season | A warmer kitchen or warmer months | **Destroyer** if the timing matches | Fermentation rate depends steeply on temperature, so this alone can explain the thriving. Checking a thermometer reading before and after the jazz began settles it. |
| Flour, ratio, water, vessel changes | Any change coinciding with the jazz | Weakener each, destroyer in combination | One change is one assumption to revise. Several at once mean the comparison has to be rebuilt from scratch. |
| Regression to the mean | The jazz began during a slump | Weakener | Revise: a recovery is expected without treatment. The size of the recovery beyond the usual bounce needs a baseline to judge. |
| No known mechanism for audible sound | Absence of a mechanism I can cite (unverified here) | Weakener | Lowers the prior but does not falsify. Unexplained effects sometimes exist. Vibration through the vessel remains a candidate to test. |
| Genre-specificity | No reason jazz differs from other sound at equal volume | Weakener | Narrows the claim from "jazz" to "sound or vibration", which is a revision to one assumption and not a rebuild. |
| Unfalsifiable as stated | Any bad day gets explained away | **Destroyer** of the claim as worded | Rebuild as a falsifiable claim with a stated failure condition, for example "the jazz jar peaks at least an hour earlier than the silent jar on most days across two weeks". |
| Matched silent jar performs equally | The two-jar experiment | **Destroyer** | Would end the claim outright. This is the strongest rebutting defeater and the one to run first. |

**Overall standing.** The observation, that the starter is doing well, is plausible and untouched. The causal claim has several sufficient rival explanations (attention, temperature, season, recovery from a slump), any one of which produces the same result, and no direct evidence tying the effect to the music. As it stands, the claim is a weakened hypothesis and not a conclusion. Two defeaters, temperature and caretaker attention, would each destroy it if the timing lines up, and both can be checked from what the household already knows (when the jazz began, when feeding habits or the room's temperature changed).

**Cheapest test that most moves the estimate:** two matched jars, one with jazz and one silent, on the same shelf, for two weeks, with rise height marked on each. If the jazz jar wins consistently, the claim is worth chasing. If the two jars match, the starter is thriving for reasons the household already provides, and the music is for the household.
