# argue-the-opposite

Stress-test a position by building the strongest counter-case from a capable skeptic's view, conceding where the opposition is right, then deciding whether to refine, abandon, or hold.

## Call on it when

- You hold a position and are about to act on it, publish it, or defend it, and nobody has pushed back yet.
- You believe something and cannot say what the best case against it is.

## What it hands back

The position stated plainly, a counter-argument from factual, logical, practical, and historical angles, alternatives that rest on different assumptions, what the opposition gets right, and a verdict: refine, abandon, or maintain the position.

A run on "Cats are better office managers than people", trimmed ([full run](https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills/blob/main/examples/thinkies/argue-the-opposite/cats-as-office-managers.md)):

> **Logical.** The position rests on a hidden swap. It starts from "cats are pleasant to have around" and ends at "cats manage better." […] An absent fault is not a performed duty. By the same reasoning, an empty chair is a better manager than any person, since it also holds no meetings and takes no credit. If the argument proves the empty chair wins, the argument proves too much.
>
> […]
>
> ### 6. Decide
>
> Refine. […] The refined position: **a cat is a good addition to an office and a poor replacement for its manager. It beats a bad manager on mood and loses to any competent one on everything else.**
