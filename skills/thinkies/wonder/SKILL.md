---
name: wonder
description: Use at the very start, when a situation is vague, surprising, or confusing and its direction is still open. Opens up possibilities by following curiosity from whatever is odd about it, producing questions worth pursuing.
---

The subject is the situation, thing, or problem named in the request.

## Steps

### 1. Notice the surprising

Notice what seems surprising or counterintuitive about the situation: what would be expected, and what happens instead.

### 2. Ask generative questions

Ask "what if," "what might happen," "how might." Favor a question whose answer you cannot yet guess.

### 3. Follow the threads

Follow interesting threads, letting one question lead to another: answer each provisionally, then ask what that answer raises.

### 4. Treat confusion as signal

Treat confusion as information about what deserves attention. Name each thing that refuses to make sense, and what it points at.

### 5. Start from uncertainty

Use uncertainty as a starting point, not an obstacle. Close on the questions worth carrying forward.

## Return

Return what is surprising, the questions, the threads followed, and each confusion with what it points at. Mark every provisional answer by its support: likely, plausible and untested, open, or unknown. The run ends on the questions worth carrying forward, and settles none of them.

A return on "why do people talk to their cars?", cut to the surprise, one confusion, and two carried questions:

<example>

- **Surprise.** An object with no ears receives the same social behavior we give a person, at the very moments when the behavior can do the least.
- **Confusion.** The talk persists after the speaker knows it is pointless. A mistake would stop once noticed, so the speech must be paying for itself in some way other than being heard.
- **Carried forward.** Do people talk more to a car they cannot afford to lose than to one they can replace? Is there any speech to a car that is not about trouble?

</example>
