### 1. What must be done, and at what level is the subject stated?

Name the act the subject must support: decide, explain, build, or fix. Name the level it is stated at: does it say why, what, how, or which one?

### 2. How does it read a few rungs above and a few rungs below?

Write two or three restatements above it, each saying why the rung below matters or what it is a kind of, and two or three below, each saying how or which one. One line per rung. Leave the rungs unexamined until the next step.

### 3. What does each rung let you do, and what does it hide?

For each rung, name the act it makes possible and the detail it buries or lacks.

### 4. Which rung do you act at?

Choose the rung with the least detail that still supports the act. Under time pressure, start higher. To explain a fault, work up from the lower rungs; to work out how something should function, work down from the top.
