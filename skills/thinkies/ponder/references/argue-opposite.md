### 1. State the position

State the position in a sentence or two its holder would accept, with the grounds it rests on.

### 2. Adopt the skeptic

Take the view of a capable skeptic who disagrees for reasons: someone who knows the subject and would hold the other side in good faith. Say who that skeptic is and what they grant before they object.

### 3. Build the counter-argument

Build the strongest case against the position from four angles: factual (what the evidence shows against it), logical (where an inference fails, or proves more than its holder would accept), practical (what goes wrong when it is acted on), and historical (what followed where something like it was tried). Give each objection the fact or inference it rests on. Where an angle yields no real objection, say so and move on.

### 4. Present alternatives

Give the positions the skeptic would hold in its place, each with the assumption it changes.

### 5. Concede what's right

Name each point where the opposition holds, and the part of the position it removes or weakens. Name also what the opposition failed to dislodge.

### 6. Decide

Choose one of refine, abandon, or maintain, from what the opposition revealed. To refine, restate the position with the conceded points built in. To abandon, name the alternative that replaces it. To maintain, name the strongest objection and why it fails.
