# The Lighthouse Keeper's Ledger — working draft

## Outline

1. **The Count.** Mara, sixteen, keeps the tally of ships for her father, the keeper of the Harrow Point light. She finds a ship in the ledger for a night she knows the light was dark.
2. **The Dark Night.** Mara learns why the light went out that night and who asked her father to let it.
3. **The Entry.** Mara decides whether to correct the ledger, and what the correction will cost her father.

## Chapter 1: The Count

The ledger lived on the shelf above the stove, where the steam kept its pages soft. Mara had kept it since she was twelve. Every ship that passed Harrow Point went in: name if she could read it, flag if she could not, the hour, the weather.

Her father trusted the count more than he trusted the lamp. The lamp could fail. The count, he said, only failed if she did.

On the first evening of spring she turned back through the winter pages, looking for the date of the storm that had taken the boathouse roof. She found it: the ninth of February. And beneath it, in her own hand, a line she did not remember writing. *Merchant, two masts, no flag. Half past two.*

She remembered the ninth. She remembered it because the light had been dark. Her father had come down from the tower at midnight and told her to go to bed, and when she asked why the lamp was out he said the wick had burned through and he would see to it at dawn.

A ship could not be counted by a light that was not burning. And she had not been awake at half past two.

She closed the ledger and set it back above the stove. Upstairs, her father's boots crossed the floor of the tower, slow and even, the way they always did.
