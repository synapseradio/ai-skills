The Bus Stop

Every weekday at 7:40 the same six people wait at the stop outside the bakery. Nobody speaks. Each stands at the same spot on the curb, as if the places were assigned.

In winter the line tightens. The man with the grey scarf moves half a step closer to the shelter, and the student behind him moves too, and by February the six stand shoulder to shoulder without having agreed to anything.

In the mountains above my grandmother's village, the beekeepers say a hive in cold weather forms a ball. The bees at the edge shiver to make heat, and every so often they trade places with the bees inside, so that no bee stays on the outside for long.

On the last cold morning of the year the grey scarf was missing. The student stood in his place, at the edge, and the rest of us shifted in.
