# The Watch — working draft

## Where it goes

Arthur tells the whole story. He presents himself as the careful brother who kept the family together while his sister Nell fell apart. He lies from the first line: he took the watch, he stopped visiting their mother, and he never paid for the funeral. None of this is stated; it shows only in what other people say to him and what he skips over. The last scene lets the audience see it all at once.

Next scene: Arthur visits Nell in hospital, telling the audience he has come to give their father's watch back to her.

## Scene 1

I have always been the one who shows up. Ask anyone. When Mother's hip went, I was the one who drove down every Sunday, rain or not, and sat with her through the long afternoons while she talked about the old house.

Nell was busy, of course. Nell is always busy.

The funeral was small, the way Mother wanted it. I took care of everything: the flowers, the hymns, the bill. At the wake Mrs Pryce from the flat downstairs held my hand far too long and said, "We did wonder where you'd got to, these last two years." Grief makes people muddled. I thanked her for coming.

Father's watch was not among Mother's things. Nell must have taken it, the way she took the good china when we cleared the house the first time. I said nothing. Somebody in this family has to be the bigger person.

It is in my coat pocket now. I keep it wound.
