# Baseline

Read this every run. The questions below derive from the alignment state in the piece's alignment file, one or two for each ring of the wheel in [align](./align.md), then four for authorship. In Score, answer each in your own words, pointing to the draft. "Nothing here" is an ordinary answer.

## Ring questions

Step 3 may drop any of these the recorded form answers, each with a reason line naming the feature of the form that answers it. Where one feature answers several questions for the same reason, one line may cover the group, naming the feature and listing each question it covers.

1. What does the draft make of what brought the piece on?
2. How does the draft stand in the setting recorded, such as the relation between the parties, the place, and whom it addresses?
3. What does the draft take this audience to bring with them?
4. What purpose does the draft take this audience to meet it with?
5. Where does the draft take its audience by its end?
6. By what path does the draft take them there, direct or arrived at?
7. How does the draft use the material recorded?
8. How does the draft stand toward the conventions of the form and tradition recorded?
9. Who speaks in the draft, from where, and in what sound?
10. What ground does the draft cover, in what parts and order?

## Authorship questions

Step 3 never drops these.

1. Wherever you changed or cut the user's words, set theirs beside yours: what moved, if anything, in meaning, register, or sound?
2. How does the draft carry each pattern the user chose, such as an order, a repeated word or phrase, or a convention of the form their text follows?
3. Where does each fact or detail the user gave stand in the draft?
4. What will the audience take from the draft as fact, as the user's intent, or as subtext, and where does each come from: the user's words, an invention the user asked for, or you?

Set each answer beside the alignment record. Where they differ, the difference goes to redrafting, or, where it touches authorship, to the user.

## The authorship line

One line holds in every form: the user authors the meaning. Add, drop, or change nothing in the user's meaning where the user cannot see it. Report each change at the detail the user asked for: a short summary to a user who says "just fix it", each change to a user who asks what changed. A change of meaning is never silent, at any detail. Where an answer to questions 11 to 14 shows something the user's words do not carry, restore the user's words, cut what you added, or name the change to the user and let them choose.

The artifact carries no gap, bracket, placeholder, or note of yours, short pieces included. An open choice lives in the state files and goes to the user.

**Contrast: a short summary that names a change of meaning**

- antipattern: "Fixed the typos and tightened the wording."
- pattern: "Fixed the typos and tightened the wording. One change of meaning: “we'll try to come” now reads “we'll come”; say if you want the first back."
- observe: the pattern adds the one edit that turned a maybe into a promise.
- shared: both are summaries at the detail a user who said "just fix it" asked for.
- contrast: whether the change of meaning is named.
- look for: an edit that moved a promise, a fact, or a stance while the summary's words passed over it.

**Contrast: a missing fact asked before drafting**

- antipattern: a draft email reading "The launch is on 14 March."
- pattern: before drafting, "Which date is the launch? The email opens on it."
- observe: the antipattern invents a date the audience will take as the user's; the pattern asks, and the draft waits for the answer.
- shared: the same email, the same need for a date.
- contrast: whether the choice reaches the user before it reaches the draft.
- look for: any date, figure, name, or event in a draft that the user neither gave nor let you invent.
