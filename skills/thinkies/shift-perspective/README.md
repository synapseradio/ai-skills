# shift-perspective

Inhabit 2–4 contrasting frames — stakeholders, disciplines, time horizons, levels of expertise — to locate what each sees that the others miss and return with insight no single frame would produce.

## When it helps

- A decision is being made from one seat, and the people it lands on weren't in the room.
- Everyone agrees, and you suspect they all stand in the same place.
- You need to find where interests actually clash, as opposed to where they only seem to.

## What comes back

The frame the question arrived in, each contrasting frame spoken from the inside in the first person (what it assumes, prioritizes, and protects), what each sees that the others miss, the clashes that are real and the ones that turn out not to be, and the insight that only shows when the frames sit side by side.

Excerpts from a run on a town square the council wants to "liven up", seen as a pigeon, as the street-sweeper who starts at 5 a.m., and as the café owner on its north side ([full run](https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills/blob/main/examples/thinkies/shift-perspective/town-square.md)):

> I know that every event leaves a debris pattern, and that the debris is what an event actually costs. A market leaves crates and wet cardboard. A concert leaves cups across the whole surface. A light show leaves nothing.
>
> […]
>
> "Livening up" the square is a decision about who carries the cost of liveliness, and no version of the plan has said so.
>
> […]
>
> An unresolved question the run leaves open: whether the square is dull at all, and to whom. The council says so. No one inside the square, from the pigeon to the owner, said it.

## Install

```bash
npx skills add https://github.com/synapseradio/ai-skills
```

Or copy `skills/thinkies/shift-perspective/` into `~/.claude/skills/shift-perspective/`.

## Usage

```
/shift-perspective <situation or decision>
```
