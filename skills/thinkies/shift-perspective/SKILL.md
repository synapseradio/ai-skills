---
name: shift-perspective
description: Use when a problem has been seen from one seat only. Takes up contrasting viewpoints, such as other stakeholders, disciplines, and horizons, to find what each sees that the rest miss and where they truly clash.
---

The subject is the situation or decision named in the request.

## Steps

### 1. Identify the current frame

Name the frame the subject arrived in: whose seat it is seen from, and what that seat takes the goal to be.

### 2. Choose contrasting perspectives

Choose a few — different stakeholders, disciplines, time horizons, or levels of expertise. Pick ones that differ from the current frame and from each other. Where the request names the perspectives, use those.

### 3. Inhabit each fully

What does it assume, prioritize, and protect? Use "I" statements from within each frame.

### 4. Find the blind spots

Locate what each perspective sees that the others miss, the starting frame included.

### 5. Note the clashes

Note where perspectives genuinely clash: where giving one what it wants costs another. Set apart the clashes that dissolve once both sides are stated.

### 6. Return with insight

Return with the insight that no single frame would have produced alone, and say what follows from it for the subject.

## Return

Return the starting frame, each perspective spoken in the first person, what each sees that the others miss, the clashes, and the insight. The run ends at the insight, with any question the frames leave open named after it.

A return on a town square the council wants to "liven up", seen as the street-sweeper who starts at 5 a.m., cut to one voice, one clash, and the insight:

<example>

- **The street-sweeper.** I know that every event leaves a debris pattern, and that the debris is what an event actually costs. A market leaves crates and wet cardboard. A concert leaves cups across the whole surface. A light show leaves nothing.
- **Clash: crowd versus cleanup.** The café owner wants a bigger crowd. The sweeper receives that crowd's waste at 5 a.m. with the same crew.
- **Insight.** "Livening up" the square is a decision about who carries the cost of liveliness, and no version of the plan has said so.

</example>
